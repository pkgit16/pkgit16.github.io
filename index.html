Research

Themes

My group studies how collective behaviour emerges in systems driven away from equilibrium, where detailed balance fails and familiar thermodynamic tools no longer apply directly. We combine exact solutions of lattice models, scaling theory and large-scale simulation, and we carry these ideas into problems from biology.

Self-organized criticality and absorbing transitions

Absorbing phase transitions beyond directed percolation and their link to SOC. We showed that fixed-energy sandpiles belong to the directed percolation class, that SOC criticality is controlled by dissipation, and that sandpiles connect firmly to linear interface models.

PRL 2002 · PRL 2012 · EPL 2014 · PRE 2016

Non-equilibrium thermodynamics

For conserved systems with short-range correlations an additive free-energy function always exists, which explains the Gamma-like mass distributions seen in mass-transport models. We also derived a condition for a zeroth law between steady states in contact, and found position–momentum uncertainty Δx Δp ≥ |ℓ|/2 in classical systems with conserved angular momentum.

PRL 2014 · PRE 2015 · PRE 2025

Motility-induced phase separation

Lattice models of run-and-tumble particles show that MIPS cannot occur in one dimension, that motility hinders clustering under attraction, and that translational diffusion is essential. Together these results place MIPS in the Ising super-universality class.

SciPost Phys 2023 · PRB 2023 · JSTAT 2024 · Soft Matter 2024

Hyperuniformity

States that are disordered at small scales but ordered at large ones, where the variance of a local observable grows as Vα with 0 < α < 1. We have found hyperuniformity in self-organized critical systems and in the critical Ashkin–Teller model, and studied percolation of hyperuniform configurations.

PRE 2016 · JPCM 2024 · PRE 2025

Exactly solvable lattice models

Finite-range processes, asymmetric hopping and assisted-exchange models with exact steady states. They show unusual correlations, current reversal with increasing density, negative differential mobility and phase separation, even in one dimension.

PRE 2015 · JSTAT 2017 · PRE 2018

Biological networks and spinal cord injury

Protein interaction networks of wild-type and mutant HTT and TP53 reveal modules whose functions may be lost or gained by mutation. With clinicians we analyse cerebrospinal fluid proteins after spinal cord injury and propose candidates that could hasten recovery.

PLoS ONE 2013 · PLoS ONE 2014 · Mol. Cell. Biochem. 2016
